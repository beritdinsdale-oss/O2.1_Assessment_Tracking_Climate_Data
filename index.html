Weather and Climate: What Does the Corvallis Graph Show?

Use a long-term temperature graph to distinguish year-to-year weather variability from a climate trend.

Learning goal

By the end of this activity, you will be able to explain how temperatures can vary from year to year while still showing a long-term climate pattern.

1. Watch: How to explore a weather station

2. Examine the Corvallis graph

Weather variability

Short-term differences between days, seasons, or individual years.

Climate trend

The broader direction or pattern that becomes visible across many years or decades.

3. Check your understanding

Select one answer for each question. You will receive immediate feedback.

Question 1. The graph rises and falls from one year to the next. What do these changes best represent?
Question 2. Suppose the next year on the graph is cooler than the year before it. What can we conclude?
Question 3. What is the best way to decide whether the local climate is changing?
Question 4. A gardener says, “Last year was cooler than the year before, so climate change is not happening.” Which response is best supported by the graph?

4. Explore a station near you

Open xmACIS in a new tab and repeat the process using a weather station near your garden or community.

As you explore, consider:

  • How much do individual years vary?
  • What broad pattern do you notice across the full record?
  • Why would one unusually hot or cool year be insufficient evidence by itself?
  • How might a long-term temperature shift affect planting dates, heat stress, frost risk, or water needs?
