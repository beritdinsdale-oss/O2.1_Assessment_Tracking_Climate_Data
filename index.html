This time, you’ll find the data.

In Module 1, we provided the weather and climate data and asked you to interpret it. In this module, you’ll learn how to find climate data yourself.

First, you’ll practice with Corvallis. Then you’ll use the same tools to investigate climate changes near your own garden.

Step 3 · Guided research

What has changed in Corvallis?

Now you’ll use the climate record to answer questions we did not answer in Module 1. Use the video as a guide and move between xmACIS and this page.

01

Temperature over time

Use a Seasonal Time Series to look at annual average temperature across the longest reliable period you can display.

What does the long-term record suggest?
02

Precipitation over time

Switch the time series to total precipitation. Look across the long record rather than focusing on one wet or dry year.

How would you describe the long-term pattern?
03

Last spring freeze over time

Open the First/Last Dates product. Use minimum temperature at or below 32°F and look at the last occurrence in spring. Turn on the graph/trend option.

What do you notice about the timing of the last spring freeze?

Step 4 · Take the method home

Build a climate snapshot for your garden

Now repeat the investigation with a station near your garden or community. These are research questions—not a test. “No clear trend” and “I’m not sure” are useful findings when they match what the record shows.
